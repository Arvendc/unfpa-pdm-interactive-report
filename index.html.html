2. Rationale, Context & Operational Scope

Region XII Emergency SRHiE & GBViE Response Assessment

Humanitarian Context

Seismic Impact

8 June 2026: A 7.8-magnitude offshore earthquake struck Sarangani, causing widespread ground shaking, landslides, and soil liquefaction across parts of Region XII.

Health facilities sustained structural damage, prompting the establishment of temporary emergency maternity spaces in outdoor and tent environments.

Displacement & Vulnerability

Displacement significantly heightened risks and barriers related to Sexual & Reproductive Health (SRH), Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), Gender-Based Violence (GBV) protection, sanitation, and maternal/newborn care.

53.0% (170) of surveyed households were large family units (6+ members) with a 127.6% household dependency ratio, markedly increasing domestic care burdens on female primary caregivers.

UNFPA Emergency Response Framework

Grounded in UNFPA’s 9th Country Programme (9CP) and operating across the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, UNFPA, in close partnership with the Department of Health (DOH), Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Integrated Provincial Health Offices (IPHOs), and partner UNYPHIL-Women, deployed multi-faceted emergency assistance:

Dignity Kits
  • Essential hygiene and menstrual health supplies
  • Protective items (flashlight, whistle), undergarments, slippers, and traditional malong
  • Targeted to WRAs, adolescent girls, female caregivers, GBV survivors, and PWDs
Maternity Packs
  • Newborn clothing, receiving blankets, cloth diapers, and postpartum sanitary supplies
  • Targeted to pregnant and lactating women in evacuation communities and health facility wards
Emergency Maternity Tents
  • Deployed Emergency Maternity Tent Facilities (EMTFs)
  • Provided temporary clinical spaces supporting labor, safe delivery, and immediate postpartum care at damaged health facilities

Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) Core Objectives

Assessment Window: 3–8 August 2026

The field monitoring deployment systematically examined five core evaluation pillars:

1. Coverage & Reach

Did assistance successfully reach intended vulnerable groups (PLWs, WRAs, PWDs, adolescents, IP communities)?

2. Timeliness & Logistics

Was distribution timely, physically accessible, safe, and well-managed across venues and hospital wards?

3. Quality & Utility

Were kit commodities complete, undamaged, culturally appropriate, and actively utilized by recipients?

4. Protection & AAP

Were assistance, venues, and feedback channels unconditional, safe, inclusive, confidential, and accountable?

5. Action Planning

What operational gaps, lessons, and joint action points should guide ongoing recovery and future disaster preparedness?

3. Methodology & Operational Approach

Region XII Emergency Response Assessment | August 2026 PDM Deployment

3.1 Study Design

3.1.1 Mixed-Methods Approach

The evaluation utilized a concurrent mixed-methods design to triangulate quantitative trends with qualitative lived experiences across displaced communities and health facilities:

  • 321 Surveys Validated individual & household quantitative survey interviews.
  • 8 FGDs / 62 Participants Age-disaggregated focus groups (Youth & Adult cohorts).
  • Informal Interviews Engagements with RHU health personnel, BHWs, evacuation focal points & community leaders.

3.2 Quantitative Sampling

3.2.1 Sampling Frame:

1,583 registered assistance packages (922 Dignity Kits & 661 Maternity Packs).

3.2.2 Sample Parameters:
  • Target Sample: 351 respondents | Validated Sample: 321 respondents
  • Statistical Standard: 95% Confidence Level, 10% Margin of Error
3.2.3 Geographic Coverage:

Four priority municipalities in Region XII: Glan, Malapatan, Alabel (Sarangani) and Lake Sebu (South Cotabato).

3.3 Qualitative Sampling

3.3.1 Focus Group Discussions (8 sessions / 62 participants):
  • Youth Cohort (15–24 yrs): 4 sessions, 28 participants
  • Adult Cohort (25+ yrs): 4 sessions, 34 participants
3.3.2 Key Discussion Areas:

SRH & MHM needs, earthquake water access constraints, maternal care integration, kit commodity utility, physical mobility barriers, and AAP/feedback mechanism preferences.

3.4 Data Collection & Field Deployment

3.4.1 Data Collection Period

3–8 August 2026 active field monitoring deployment across coastal, upland, and hospital ward sites.

3.4.2 Digital Survey Tool

KoboToolbox mobile application featuring automated skip logic and mandatory validation controls.

3.4.3 Field Deployment Team

8 local bilingual enumerators, 4 qualitative FGD facilitators, 1 IP community coordinator, and UNFPA M&E/humanitarian officers.

3.5 Ethics & Do-No-Harm

3.5.1 Participant Protection:
  • Explicit informed verbal consent establishing unconditionality of assistance.
  • Strict data confidentiality and complete anonymization.
  • Safe, private, and culturally adapted data collection spaces.
  • Trauma-informed facilitation in local dialects (Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Blaan).
3.5.2 Referral & Protection Pathways:

Active referral linkages with LSWDO social workers and RHU health officers for GBV protection concerns, maternal health complications, and acute pediatric distress.

3.6 Scope & Limitations

3.6.1 Assessment Scope:

Evaluated Dignity Kits, Maternity Packs, and Emergency Maternity Tent Facilities (EMTFs).

3.6.2 Geographic Limitation:

Restricted to four target municipalities in Region XII; findings reflect monitored sites and are not generalizable across all earthquake-affected regions.

3.6.3 Methodological Limitations:

Retrospective recall bias, incomplete beneficiary registries at baseline, enumeration travel delays in mountain sitios, smaller facility-based samples (Alabel), and telecommunications outages in Glan and Lake Sebu.

5. Key Challenges & Lessons Learned

5.1 Operational & Logistical Challenges

Transit & Access Geographic Isolation & Mountain Transit (Lake Sebu & Glan)

Landslide-damaged trails restricted vehicle access, requiring multi-stage transit via motorcycles (habal-habal) and foot travel to reach remote indigenous sitios.

Supply Redundancy Clinical Supply Redundancies in Hospital Packs

Distributing standard Maternity Packs in health facility wards created supply overlaps (e.g., umbilical cord clamps), indicating a need for distinct facility versus community kit configurations.

Quality Assurance Pre-Dispatch Kitting Quality Control

Item omission rates (16.9% incomplete Dignity Kits; 24.6% incomplete Maternity Packs) occurred during rapid kitting operations without standardized checklist verification.

5.2 Protection & AAP Bottlenecks

Telecom Outages Cellular Network Outages & Hotline Responsiveness

Telecom outages and unresponsive telephone hotlines in Glan affected digital grievance channels, leading beneficiaries to rely on non-digital alternatives.

Feedback Gap Follow-up Reporting Gap

All complaint filers (100.0% or 60) reported receiving no follow-up information regarding actions taken, representing an operational gap in closed-loop communication.

Item Specification Undergarment Fit Mismatches

Underwired bras included in Dignity Kits caused discomfort for pregnant and lactating women, resulting in an 8.0% non-utility rate.

5.3 Strategic Lessons Learned

WASH & MHM Water Access Impacting MHM Preferences

Earthquake damage to water supply lines hindered the rewashing of reusable cloth pads, driving a 100.0% beneficiary preference for disposable sanitary napkins.

Methodology Mixed-Methods Data Triangulation

Combining quantitative KoboToolbox surveys with age-disaggregated FGDs was necessary to identify protection dynamics, undergarment mismatches, and AAP trust preferences.

Modality Alignment Cash-NFI Complementarity

NFI kits fulfill immediate material needs, but complementary Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPC) is required to address shifting household demands for fresh food, infant milk, and pediatric medicines.

6. Recommendations & Joint Action Plan

6.1 Operational Priorities for the UNFPA Humanitarian Team

1. Supply Chain Calibration & Context-Specific Kit Customization
  • Facility vs. Community Split: Remove umbilical cord clamps from hospital-bound Maternity Packs to eliminate duplication with pre-existing health ward stock.
  • Pediatric Item Expansion: Reallocate procurement savings to incorporate manual breast pumps, pediatric fever medicine (infant Paracetamol 0-12 months), baby wipes, and higher quantities of disposable diapers into community-level distributions.
  • Undergarment Fit Revision: Adjust undergarment procurement specifications to replace underwired bras with soft, stretchable, wireless maternity/nursing bras or free-size cotton undergarments.
  • Distribution Documentation Checklists & Templates: Provide Integrated Provincial Health Offices (IPHOs) and Rural Health Units (RHUs) with standardized templates and verification checklists necessary to accurately document and report distributions.
Responsible: UNFPA Humanitarian Team & Procurement Unit
2. Multi-Channel AAP Mechanism Optimization
  • Hotline Restoration & Local Adaptation: Troubleshoot telephone hotline responsiveness in Glan. In areas with weak cellular coverage, utilize physical complaint boxes as primary reporting nodes.
  • Youth-Centric Anonymous Channels: Expand physical, lockable complaint boxes in youth-dense environments (schools, youth centers, evacuation camps) and establish direct SMS lines with BHW youth coordinators.
  • Closed-Loop Tracking Protocol: Institutionalize a central Feedback Log & Tracker across distribution partners to ensure closed-loop communication and status feedback to complainants within 14 days.
Responsible: AAP Focal Point & M&E Unit
3. Cash & Voucher Assistance (CVA) Integration
  • Complementary Cash Pilots: Conduct market feasibility assessments to introduce multipurpose cash assistance alongside NFI kit distributions, providing postpartum mothers financial flexibility to purchase fresh food, infant formula, and pediatric supplies.
Responsible: Humanitarian Team & Cash Working Group Focal Point

6.2 Practical Field Alignment (UNFPA, DOH, and DSWD)

1. DOH-UNFPA SRH Sub-Cluster Coordination
  • Local SRHiE Inventory Tracking: Align UNFPA kit releases with DOH Regional Office XII and Provincial Health Office (PHO) inventory tracking to prevent facility supply duplication and ensure replenishment of maternal supplies in health facilities.
  • Trauma-Informed BHW Capacity Building: Conduct orientation refreshers for DOH/LGU-supported Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) focusing on adolescent-friendly interaction, non-verbal distress identification, and referral of postpartum and GBV complications.
Responsible: DOH Regional Office XII, PHOs, & RHUs
2. Practical MSWDO / LSWDO Local Relief Coordination
  • Beneficiary Roster Validation: Coordinate with Municipal Social Welfare and Development Offices (MSWDO) during pre-distribution planning to cross-reference beneficiary registries against local emergency lists, prioritizing unassisted pregnant/lactating women and PWDs.
  • Ground-Level Referral Linkages: Establish clear referral steps with local social workers so that mothers needing supplementary food or cash assistance identified during kit distributions are flagged directly to MSWDO relief channels.
Responsible: MSWDO / LSWDO & Field Implementation Teams
3. Grounded Inter-Agency Protection & Feedback Integration
  • Utilizing Existing RHU & WFS Help Desks: Utilize existing Rural Health Unit (RHU) desks and LGU Women-Friendly Spaces (WFS) for feedback intake rather than establishing standalone inter-agency desks during rapid recovery phases.
  • Protection Pathway Alignment: Channel sensitive protection feedback from complaint boxes and hotlines directly into local GBV Sub-Cluster and Local Council for the Protection of Children (LCPC) referral pathways managed by MSWDO and municipal health officers.
Responsible: GBV Sub-Cluster, LCPC, & Municipal Health Officers
